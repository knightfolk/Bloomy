# BloomGauge and Bloomy comparison — October 7, 2026

Bloomy has a strong foundation for a native Mac control app. BloomGauge's clearest advantage is explaining earning outcomes: what happened during a day, why a model stays loaded, and why a Mac is not receiving work. The useful direction is to improve Bloomy's data attribution and explanations while retaining SwiftUI/AppKit.

This is a research review, not a comparative performance or earnings benchmark. Bloomy source was inspected at `fa6d6abd8c8ffbdda8cbc2eb1452985e7151467f`. A bounded native reviewer audited the current implementation. BloomGauge's site, guide, changelog, five published screenshots and twelve public core files were inspected. Its public core was pinned to `724ce679e407eba0806cd03e94f303c4fb728468`; its README says the core lags the downloadable app and excludes newer features. No browser was available, so neither its live app interactions nor its current Guardian were tested. No provider commands, inference, downloads of models, production configuration changes or application changes were performed.

The homepage advertised 1.36.73 beta 54, while the changelog's latest entry was 1.36.72 beta 53 and other pages named older versions. Treat this as a publication inconsistency, not evidence about installed behavior. Their September 29 comparison describes our former Darkbloom Control and omits current features; it is not an authoritative Bloomy comparison. [Homepage](https://bloomgauge.io/), [changelog](https://bloomgauge.io/changelog), [comparison](https://bloomgauge.io/help/darkbloom-apps-for-mac), [public core](https://github.com/cookder/bloomgauge).

**The most useful visual ideas.** The [day replay](https://bloomgauge.io/media/features/day-replay.jpg) makes model residence and gaps understandable in one timeline. Bring that into Activity: model bands, observed accepted-work/loading intervals, nudge attempts and reward arrivals, with unknown intervals visibly distinct. Selecting a segment should reveal its evidence. Completion time does not establish when a request started, and a credit arriving after a switch must not automatically be assigned to the new model.

The [demand screen](https://bloomgauge.io/media/features/model-demand.jpg) puts models on a common scale and marks the usual level. Bloomy model cards could show one compact demand bar and a sparkline, with full units and freshness in details. Preserve the network sampling interval: a raw request count from one interval is not comparable with another. Only label a profitable zone when sufficient attributable pay history supports it.

The [switch screen](https://bloomgauge.io/media/features/switch-rules.jpg) explains whether a move can recover its costs. Our simpler default could say: estimated advantage, measured loading time, estimated payback and the next eligible decision time. Keep advanced controls behind disclosure. The [dashboard](https://bloomgauge.io/images/hero-dashboard-1400.webp) has a useful financial hierarchy, but its prose-heavy cards and multiple navigation rows are not a good template for our small popup. Keep our icon-first common bar and fixed geometry. Avoid celebratory credit animation while status stability and Reduce Motion proof remain open.

The [readiness chain](https://bloomgauge.io/media/features/why-not-earning.jpg) provides a useful diagnosis format. For Bloomy, use independently evaluated states with evidence timestamps: connected, authorized, accepting work, model ready, work observed and credits received. Present the first actionable problem and one relevant action. Network quiet, a long accepted request, stale telemetry and a failed local provider must have different explanations. Do not encode the screenshot's chain as an authoritative Darkbloom payout specification without checking current official behavior.

**First fix: provider-aware money records.** [AccountEarnings.swift](../../Sources/DarkbloomTelemetry/AccountEarnings.swift) decodes earning ID, provider identity, model, integer amount, tokens and timestamp on each earning; account identity is on the enclosing response. [EarningsDatabase.swift](../../Sources/DarkbloomTelemetry/EarningsDatabase.swift) stores work by hour/model and uses one maximum-ID watermark. Provider identity and account boundaries are lost in that aggregate. An older-ID late arrival or correction can be excluded. Combining those account totals with local active time cannot establish this Mac's measured earning rate. The existing [attribution review](STAT_ATTRIBUTION_REVIEW.md) describes this same unresolved issue.

Persist a bounded ledger keyed by `(account scope, earning ID)` with provider/model dimensions, transactional correction handling and coverage. Derive account/provider/model totals from it and reconcile them with authoritative account totals. Map this Mac to a provider only with verified evidence. Preserve old hourly buckets as legacy account aggregates. Tests should cover overlapping pages, late records, signed corrections, account changes, incomplete history and model switches. This is a plausible data-loss mechanism to investigate, not proof that it caused Kevin's earlier missing rewards. Expected five-minute intervals must never fabricate paid credits.

This finding applies to earnings aggregates. Action History already derives an event identity from account and earning IDs, but does not provide queryable account/provider dimensions or a correction-aware money ledger, and excludes negative earning rows. A correction issued as a new higher earning ID can reach the existing earnings aggregates; changing an already-seen record cannot update them. Keep action evidence and financial ingestion distinct, and do not persist credential-bearing fields unnecessarily.

BloomGauge's inspected `native/history.py` uses account/earning-ID deduplication, a useful structural comparison. That public credits table also omits provider identity, although `live_earnings.py` parses it. Its session/readiness tracking adds evidence, but public source does not establish perfect machine attribution. Do not assume its financial labels are a gold standard.

**Next fix: join facts into a day and switch timeline.** Reuse [ModelVisitHistory.swift](../../Sources/DarkbloomTelemetry/ModelVisitHistory.swift), performance history, actions and the new ledger. Preserve their different scopes. Show loaded-but-no-observed-work residence, readiness coverage, request/counter uncertainty at switches, credits and energy coverage. Keep no observed work distinct from confirmed zero work. A before/after comparison can describe measured outcomes; it cannot prove what staying on the previous model would have earned.

**Then improve decision evidence.** [ProfitSwitchPolicy.swift](../../Sources/DarkbloomTelemetry/ProfitSwitchPolicy.swift) already requires a supported estimated next-hour advantage after loading: at least 30% and $0.05 when the incumbent projection is positive; when that projection is zero, percentage gain is undefined and the $0.05 floor applies. Defaults include ten-minute confirmation, sixty-minute residence, a three-hour return wait, five-minute loading allowance and three attempts in a rolling 24-hour window. This already addresses ping-pong; another independent controller would add risk.

Audit two conservative prerequisites with pure fixtures: the incumbent must have positive calibrated net income, and an alternative must exceed both its raw request count and gross earning rate. Those checks can exclude a better normalized or lower-electricity net alternative. They are policy findings, not demonstrated financial harm. Add explicit round-trip loading cost, uncertainty and measured post-switch outcomes before changing live eligibility. Estimate payback only when a supported positive net-rate difference exists; mark unsupported inputs unavailable. Keep simulated replay separate from earned money. Bloomy's recommendation replay currently reevaluates stored evidence, not a financial backtest.

**Keep the native stack; improve shared foundations.** BloomGauge's [architecture](https://github.com/cookder/bloomgauge/blob/main/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md) describes Swift/WKWebView, bundled Python, React/Vite, SQLite and a Tailscale phone listener. That offers reuse across Mac and phone, with more runtime and HTTP boundaries to maintain. Bloomy's [package](../../Package.swift) already separates native UI, telemetry, companion protocol, host and transport. There is no evidence supporting a frontend rewrite or a claim that Swift alone makes us faster.

Prioritize one shared evidence model for readiness and freshness; one coordinated command owner across manual controls, Autopilot, profit switching and GPU protection; and transactional ledger ingestion. Extend existing SQLite stores rather than introducing another backend. Retain compact long-term rollups while bounding raw samples. Audit currently unbounded hourly earnings growth and the in-memory failed-write backlog. Keep hidden charts unloaded and sampling frequency tied to monitoring needs rather than animation.

For a meaningful resource claim, measure comparable release builds: process-tree CPU, memory, wakeups, database growth and launch/refresh latency, both visible and hidden, with matched history sizes and provider conditions. Existing inert fixture improvements are useful but do not prove comparative battery savings. Current production menu-ring lifecycle proof also remains open.

**Delivery order.** Provider-aware ledger first; joined day/switch timeline and readiness explanations second; bounded demand history and compact card graphics third; economic policy audit fourth; resumable official-provider setup fifth; then complete signed companion packaging, registration and physical-device validation. General provider recovery should respect Darkbloom's existing recovery ownership and manual stops. Current GPU protection is not a generic crash supervisor. Keep long requests protected: their credits arrive at completion, so silence alone is insufficient evidence for restart. BloomGauge's [October 2 changelog](https://bloomgauge.io/changelog) documents a fix in this area.

Implement original Swift components and independently specified behavior.
